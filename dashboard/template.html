Receiver and coverage scouting

NFL Big Data Bowl 2026 · 2023 season

Every targeted pass of the 2023 NFL season, measured twice: once at the release, where separation and coverage leverage are set, and once through the flight, the half second to two seconds while the ball is in the air and a receiver and a defender are both reacting to a destination only one of them chose. The flight is where this data is unusual - it is the only window in which you can watch two players respond to the same known landing spot and compare what each did about it.

The play runs from the snap. Everybody on the field is tracked up to the throw and their positions are recorded every three tenths of a second, so the run‑up is drawn between samples; the ball in the air is every tenth of a second, for the players the tracking follows there. The clock is zero at the release — it counts down through the route and up through the flight.

Each number carries its percentile among every target of the season that has one, shaded blue where the play sits low and red where it sits high. The chip says where the number falls, not whether it is good: a receiver’s separation and a defender’s closing speed are both better high, and both are in every play here, so the metrics are grouped by whose they are instead — in the colours those players are drawn in above.

Matching plays

Separation is not comparable across positions. A back released into the flat is open by ten yards because nobody is covering him there; a receiver running a go route is open by two because a corner is running with him. Filter to one position before reading the separation columns as a ranking. Catch rate over expected is already adjusted for the throw, so it does compare.

Coverage is charged to the defender nearest the receiver when the ball is released, which is the same proxy Next Gen Stats uses for separation. On a bracket it names one of the two defenders, and on a busted zone exchange it can name the man who was closest rather than the man who was beaten. Passer rating allowed is the standard formula applied to the throws he was charged with.

Calibration, out of fold

Plays are grouped into ten equal buckets by predicted catch probability. A point on the diagonal means that when the model said 40%, 40% of those throws were caught. Calibration is what makes catch rate over expected meaningful: without it the residual measures the model's bias rather than the receiver's hands.

What the model is beating

Validation is grouped by game, so no play is scored by a model that saw another snap from the same game. Plays from one game share a quarterback, a secondary and a weather, and a random split would let the model recognise the game rather than the throw.

What it leans on

Permutation importance: how much worse the log loss gets when one feature is shuffled. The model sees only the release - where everyone was and how they were moving when the ball left the hand, plus where it was going and how long it would be in the air. Nothing from the flight is a feature, which is checked in the test suite rather than trusted.
